Assertion–Reason Questions
Direction: In the following questions, a statement of assertion (A) is followed by a statement of reason (R). Mark the correct choice as:
(a) Both assertion (A) and reason (R) are true and reason (R) is the correct explanation of assertion (A).
(b) Both assertion (A) and reason (R) are true but reason (R) is not the correct explanation of assertion (A).
(c) Assertion (A) is true but reason (R) is false.
(d) Assertion (A) is false but reason (R) is true.
Q1. Assertion (A): A 10 m vertical pole casting a 10 m shadow indicates a solar elevation of 45°.
Reason (R): For a vertical pole, tan θ = height/shadow, and tan 45° = 1.
Q2. Assertion (A): A 12 m ladder inclined at 60° to level ground reaches a height of 6√3 m.
Reason (R): The angle of elevation is the angle between the horizontal and the upward line of sight.
Q3. Assertion (A): The angle of depression of a car from a tower equals the car’s angle of elevation to the tower top.
Reason (R): These two angles are vertically opposite angles.
Q4. Assertion (A): In a heights-and-distances right-triangle model, an angle of elevation may be 120°.
Reason (R): The angle of elevation is measured upward from the horizontal to the line of sight and is acute in this model.

Q5. Assertion (A): A 24 m tower viewed at an elevation of 60° is 8√3 m from the observer.
Reason (R): For a vertical tower, tan 60° = height/horizontal distance = √3.
Q6. Assertion (A): A flagstaff stands on a tower. If the elevations of the tower top and flagstaff top are 45° and 60°, the tower height equals the horizontal distance from the observer.
Reason (R): sin 60° = √3/2.
Q7. Assertion (A): A ship seen at a depression of 30° from a 30 m lighthouse is 30√3 m from its foot.
Reason (R): In a right triangle, cos 30° = opposite side/hypotenuse.
Q8. Assertion (A): The angle of depression from a tower top to a point on level ground can be −45°.
Reason (R): An angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal through the observer.
Q9. Assertion (A): Two equal poles stand 20 m apart. From their midpoint, each top has elevation 45°, so each pole is 10 m high.
Reason (R): For either right triangle, tan 45° = height/10 = 1.
Q10. Assertion (A): When a broken tree touches the ground, its fallen upper part is the hypotenuse of the right triangle formed.
Reason (R): The angle of depression equals the corresponding angle of elevation because horizontal lines are parallel.

Q11. Assertion (A): For a fixed vertical tower, an observer’s angle of elevation increases as the observer moves closer on level ground.
Reason (R): tan θ decreases as θ increases from 30° to 60°.
Q12. Assertion (A): A 20 m taut rope inclined at 30° to the ground reaches a vertical height of −10 m.
Reason (R): For this right triangle, sin 30° = vertical height/rope length = 1/2.
Q13. Assertion (A): A tower is seen at 30°. After the observer moves 20 m towards it, the elevation is 60°; the tower is 10√3 m high.
Reason (R): For a vertical object, tan θ = height/horizontal distance at each observation point.
Q14. Assertion (A): A 1.5 m tall observer standing 28.5 m from a chimney sees its top at 45°, so the chimney is 30 m high.
Reason (R): sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for an acute angle θ.
Q15. Assertion (A): Of two ships on the same side of a lighthouse, the nearer ship subtends the larger angle of depression.
Reason (R): An angle of depression is measured upward from the horizontal through the observer.
Q16. Assertion (A): For a fixed tower height, the horizontal distance is greater at an elevation of 60° than at 30°.
Reason (R): For tower height h and elevation θ, horizontal distance d = h cot θ.

Q17. Assertion (A): From a point 20 m above a lake, a cloud at 30° elevation and its reflection at 60° depression place the cloud 40 m above the lake.
Reason (R): A reflection is as far below the lake surface as the cloud is above it, and tan θ = vertical change/horizontal distance.
Q18. Assertion (A): The top of a 15 m tower viewed from 5√3 m away has an elevation of 60°.
Reason (R): The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle.
Q19. Assertion (A): At a fixed elevation of 45°, doubling the horizontal distance doubles the required vertical height.
Reason (R): tan 45° = √3.
Q20. Assertion (A): Two equal poles are seen from a point between them at 60° and 30°. The nearer and farther horizontal distances are in the ratio 1 : 3.
Reason (R): Since the poles have equal heights, the point must be equidistant from them.
