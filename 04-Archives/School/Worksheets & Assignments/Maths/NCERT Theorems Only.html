CH 1 • REAL NUMBERS — §1.2, 1.3
Real Numbers — 3 Theorems
Theorem 1.1 — Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic:
Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.
✔ IN NCERT 2026-27 — §1.2 Summary Point 1 — Most important, used for HCF/LCM
Theorem 1.2:
Let p be a prime number. If p divides a², then p divides a, where a is a positive integer.
✔ IN NCERT — §1.3 — Used to prove √2, √3 irrational
Theorem 1.3 — √2 is irrational:
√2 is irrational. Proof by contradiction: Assume √2 = a/b (coprime) → 2b² = a² → 2|a → a=2c → 2|b → contradiction. Same method proves √3, √5 and 5-√3, 3√2 are irrational (NCERT Examples 5-7).
✔ IN NCERT — §1.3 + Summary Point 3
✘ DELETED & NOT IN BOOK NOW: Euclid’s Division Lemma — “a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b” — was Theorem 1.1 in old book, now removed in rationalised edition. Some schools still ask it — learn `a=bq+r` for half-yearly just in case.
CH 2 • POLYNOMIALS — §2.2, 2.3
Polynomials — Relationship Theorems
No numbered “Theorem” in NCERT, but Relationships (proved in NCERT §2.3):
For ax²+bx+c, a≠0 with zeroes α,β:
α + β = -b/a   and   αβ = c/a
For ax³+bx²+cx+d, a≠0 with α,β,γ:
α+β+γ = -b/a , αβ+βγ+γα = c/a , αβγ = -d/a
✔ IN NCERT — §2.3 box + Summary. Verified in NCERT Exercise 2.2 solutions.
Geometrical Meaning (NCERT §2.2):
The zero of a polynomial p(x) is the x-coordinate of the point where the graph y=p(x) intersects the x-axis.
Linear: 1 zero • Quadratic: 0/1/2 • Cubic: up to 3
CH 3 • PAIR OF LINEAR EQUATIONS — §3.2
Pair of Linear Equations — Graphical Condition
Condition for Consistency (NCERT §3.2, Table):
For a₁x+b₁y+c₁=0, a₂x+b₂y+c₂=0
• a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ → One solution (intersecting) — Consistent
• a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂ → Infinitely many (coincident) — Consistent
• a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ → No solution (parallel) — Inconsistent
✔ IN NCERT — Only this table + Substitution (§3.3.1) & Elimination (§3.3.2) are theorems/methods now.
✘ DELETED: Cross-multiplication theorem and “Equations reducible to linear pair” — not in book.
CH 4 • QUADRATIC EQUATIONS — §4.3, 4.4
Quadratic Equations
Definition (NCERT §4.2):
A quadratic equation in x is of the form ax²+bx+c=0, where a,b,c are real numbers and a≠0.
Theorem — Nature of Roots (§4.4):
For ax²+bx+c=0, let D = b²-4ac
• D > 0 → Two distinct real roots: (-b ± √D)/2a
• D = 0 → Two equal real roots: -b/2a
• D < 0 → No real roots
✘ DELETED: Theorem “Solution by Completing the Square” — removed. Only Factorisation is taught in NCERT now.
CH 5 • AP — §5.2, 5.3, 5.4
Arithmetic Progressions
Definition (NCERT §5.2):
A list a₁,a₂,a₃… is an AP if a₂-a₁ = a₃-a₂ = … = d (common difference, constant).
aₙ = a + (n-1)d   •   Sₙ = n/2[2a+(n-1)d] = n/2(a+l)
✔ All theorems retained — No deletion in Ch 5.
CH 6 • TRIANGLES — §6.3, 6.4 — MOST THEOREMS (5M PROOFS)
Triangles — BPT + Similarity Criteria
Theorem 6.1 — Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT / Thales):
If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
In ΔABC, if DE ∥ BC (D on AB, E on AC) ⇒ AD/DB = AE/EC (also AD/AB = AE/AC)
✔ IN NCERT — §6.3 — Most asked 5M proof in half-yearly!
Theorem 6.2 — Converse of BPT:
If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.
If AD/DB = AE/EC ⇒ DE ∥ BC
✔ IN NCERT — §6.3 — Used to prove Mid-point theorem
Theorem 6.3 — AAA Similarity:
If in two triangles, corresponding angles are equal, then their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (and hence triangles are similar).
Corollary: AA — If 2 angles equal → triangles similar
Theorem 6.4 — SSS Similarity:
If in two triangles, sides of one triangle are proportional to sides of the other, then their corresponding angles are equal (hence similar).
Theorem 6.5 — SAS Similarity:
If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of the other and the sides including these angles are proportional, then triangles are similar.
✔ Theorems 6.3-6.5 are IN NCERT §6.4 — Criteria for Similarity — All retained in rationalised book. Learn statements word-to-word.
✘ DELETED in rationalised NCERT: Theorem 6.6 — Areas of Similar Triangles: ar1/ar2 = (side1/side2)² (§6.5 old) — deleted.
✘ DELETED: Theorem 6.8 — Pythagoras: In a right triangle, square of hypotenuse = sum of squares of other two sides: AC²=AB²+BC² (§6.6 old) + Converse — both deleted! But many schools still ask 5M proof — prepare if your half-yearly includes it.
CH 7 • COORDINATE GEOMETRY — §7.2, 7.3
Coordinate Geometry
Distance Formula (NCERT §7.2):
Distance between P(x₁,y₁) and Q(x₂,y₂) is √[(x₂-x₁)²+(y₂-y₁)²]. Derived from Pythagoras.
Section Formula (NCERT §7.3):
Point dividing join of (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂) internally in ratio m:n is ( (mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n) , (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n) ).
Mid-point = (x₁+x₂/2 , y₁+y₂/2) for m=n=1
✘ DELETED: Area of Triangle formula (§7.4) — deleted.
CH 8 • TRIGONOMETRY — §8.2, 8.4
Introduction to Trigonometry
Trigonometric Ratios (NCERT §8.2):
In right Δ, sinA=P/H, cosA=B/H, tanA=P/B, cosec=H/P, sec=H/B, cot=B/P.
Identities (NCERT §8.4):
1) sin²A + cos²A = 1
2) 1 + tan²A = sec²A
3) 1 + cot²A = cosec²A — proved from Pythagoras.
✘ DELETED: Ratios of complementary angles — deleted.
CH 9 • APPLICATIONS — §9.1
Some Applications of Trigonometry
No numbered theorem — Only definitions:
Angle of Elevation: angle between horizontal and line of sight when object is above eye.
Angle of Depression: angle between horizontal and line of sight when object is below eye.
Both measured with horizontal. Elevation = Depression (alternate angles when two horizontals are parallel).
✔ NCERT §9.1 — No theorem to prove, only application of tanθ.
CH 14 • PROBABILITY — §14.1
Probability
Definition (NCERT §14.1):
Probability of an event E: P(E) = Number of outcomes favourable to E / Number of all possible outcomes.
0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1 • P(not E)=1-P(E) • Impossible P=0, Sure P=1
✔ No theorems to prove — only this definition and examples (cards, dice).
